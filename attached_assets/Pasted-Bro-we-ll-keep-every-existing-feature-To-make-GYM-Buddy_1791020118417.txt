Bro, we’ll keep every existing feature. To make GYM Buddy more premium and advanced, I’d improve how the features look, work together, and respond—not replace them.

Here’s the upgrade direction I’d recommend.

1. Refine our existing visual identity
Keep cream backgrounds, charcoal surfaces, and lime accents, but use them more deliberately:

Charcoal for the most important progress or active-workout cards.
Lime for primary actions, selected controls, and meaningful progress.
Softer borders and fewer heavy shadows.
Consistent spacing, corner shapes, and icon sizes.
Large, readable numbers with smaller supporting labels.
A matching dark mode—not simply inverted colours.
Avoid: glass effects everywhere, excessive gradients, tiny text, or making every card visually dominant.

2. Give each screen a more polished layout
Screen	Recommended upgrade
Today	A strong daily-summary card, compact metric row, “Continue where you left off,” and shortcuts to all existing sections.
Nutrition	Clear progress toward targets, faster portion controls, recent meals, and a better organised meal history.
Strength	A dedicated active-workout view with the current exercise, previous results, next set, and rest timer.
Movement	More readable progress, clearer tracking status, and well-separated history and settings.
Insights	A weekly summary first, then all existing charts and detailed records below it.
Plan	Group related inputs, improve result presentation, and clearly explain what applying a plan changes.
Nothing disappears. Lower-priority details can be grouped into clearly labelled sections, while common actions remain easy to reach.

3. Add advanced features with everyday value
For training
Saved workout routines with an editable exercise order.
Repeat a previous session, showing previous weights and reps.
Warm-up, working, and drop-set labels where not already supported.
Adjustable rest timers with quick extensions.
Plate calculator, using your uploaded component after fixing its validation.
Exercise substitutions based on available equipment.
For nutrition
Favourite meals and saved recipes.
Repeat a meal with a different portion size.
Quick macro entry without a food search.
Barcode scanning, with nutrition values reviewed before saving.
Optional training-day and rest-day targets.
Across the app
A calendar journal combining recorded activity by date.
Customisable Today metrics and shortcuts.
Export and restore with careful duplicate handling.
Private, optional progress-photo comparisons.
These are proposals—not claims that they’re already implemented.

4. Make the interactions feel premium
Bottom sheets for quick entry; full screens for longer tasks.
Subtle haptics when a set is logged or a workout finishes.
Smooth, short transitions that respect reduced-motion settings.
Clear loading, saving, success, and error states.
Undo after deleting an entry.
Preserve form drafts and unfinished sessions.
Keep focused inputs visible when the keyboard opens.
A premium app should feel responsive without constantly animating.

5. Make reliability visible
This matters as much as appearance:

Show whether data is saved locally, syncing, or synced.
Make failed saves retryable.
Restore unfinished workouts after reopening.
Prevent invalid goals from replacing valid ones.
Confirm that every feature remains reachable through native scrolling on both platforms.
The latest scrolling change still needs native confirmation; I’d resolve that before a larger visual upgrade.
